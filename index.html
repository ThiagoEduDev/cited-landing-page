The framework

Four layers, worked in order.

The ordering is the part that makes an audit defensible. Each layer depends on the one above it — which is why a 250-item flat checklist produces paralysis instead of a plan.

[C]CrawlableCan they fetch and read it at all? Break this and nothing else matters.
[I]IdentityDo they know exactly who this is? Ambiguity gets you left out.
[T]Targeted AnswersCan a passage be lifted into an answer? Most can't.
[E]EvidenceDoes anyone independent back it up? This decides close calls.

The Scorecard sorts every failing item into this sequence automatically. That ordered list is your client deliverable — and it is the reason a client pays for an audit rather than downloading a free checklist themselves.

The story isn't obstruction — it's inattention. Almost nobody in the industry that sells search visibility has taken a position on AI crawlers. Two exceptions stood out: Trustpilot blocks every crawler site-wide, and Majestic carries a blanket Disallow: /. The Trustpilot one matters, because businesses rely on exactly that kind of third-party review page for the independent corroboration AI systems look for.

Objections, answered

The questions you are actually asking

Can't I find all this for free?
Most of the individual facts, yes. What you cannot easily assemble for free is the sequence — which of ~50 items to fix first, given that they depend on each other — and the generators that produce the actual robots.txt, llms.txt and JSON-LD. Free checklists stop at "add Organization schema". This hands you the schema. If you would rather assemble it yourself, that is genuinely a reasonable choice; it is a weekend of work.
Why not just use ChatGPT to build this?
You could get a decent checklist that way — we would expect you to. What you would not get reliably is correct, current crawler user-agent strings, JSON-LD that validates first time, the dependency ordering, or a client-ready report. You would also have no way to know which parts it got wrong. Every statistic here is sourced and dated so you can check it.
I already pay for Ahrefs / Semrush / an AI visibility tool. Do I need this?
Different job. Those tools measure — they tell you whether a site is being cited and how that trends. CITED diagnoses and fixes — it tells you why it is not being cited and generates the corrections. They complement each other. If you already pay for tracking, you have the "before" number and no process for moving it; this is that process.
Will this guarantee my client shows up in ChatGPT?
No, and be suspicious of anyone who says otherwise. These are third-party systems that are probabilistic, do not publish their selection logic, produce different answers for different users, and change without notice. What you can control is eligibility: crawler access, unambiguous identity, extractable content, third-party corroboration. Sites with those properties are cited far more often. That is a good bet, not a certainty — and saying so on a sales call tends to win business, because the client has usually just been pitched by someone promising the moon.
Is this just SEO with a new name?
There is overlap, but the target is different. SEO optimises for ranking position; this optimises for being cited inside an answer. Different signals: entity resolution, passage extractability, third-party corroboration. [FACT] Roughly 71% of pages ChatGPT cites contain structured data — that is a parsing property, not a ranking factor. And Google-Extended is a separate crawler from Googlebot, which is why sites rank fine and are still absent from AI Overviews.
Do I need to be technical?
If you already do SEO or web work, comfortably yes. The generators produce code you paste into <head>. One item may need a developer: fixing JavaScript-only rendering. Everything else is form-filling, file uploads and writing.
Is this only useful for agencies?
No — it works just as well on your own site, and plenty of buyers will use it that way. It is built for client work, which is why every check includes a verification procedure and a notes field that flows into the generated report. If you only have one site, you will still use all of it; you just will not need the bonuses.
Will it work for my client's platform?
WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, HubSpot, custom — all of them. Everything is built on open standards (robots.txt, JSON-LD, HTML semantics) that behave identically everywhere. You need a way to add code to <head> and upload a file to the site root. Every major platform supports both.
How long before the client sees results?
Week one: the score jumps and nothing changes externally — this is where most people quit. Weeks 2–6: assistants describe the business accurately when asked by name. Weeks 6–12: first unprompted mentions, usually on long-tail specific queries. Months 3–6: broader competitive queries.

Manage this expectation explicitly at the start of every engagement. "Best [category] in [city]" is a twelve-month project; "[category] in [city] that does [specific thing]" is a six-week one — and it converts better anyway.
